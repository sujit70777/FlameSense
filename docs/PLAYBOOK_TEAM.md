## 1. The project in one minute

**Challenge:** Flame in Freefall: AI-Powered Fire Safety Insights from Microgravity Combustion Data.

**The story:** On Earth, rising hot air stretches a flame into an orange teardrop. In orbit nothing
rises, so flames turn round, blue and slow. NASA now plans Moon and Mars habitats with lower pressure and
**34% oxygen** (the air we breathe is 21%), to make spacewalks faster. More oxygen makes materials burn
more easily. The first long fire tests in simulated Moon gravity only flew in 2025, and NASA's first fire tests on the Moon itself are still to come.

**What NASA asked for:** an AI-powered dashboard that **summarizes, ranks and interprets** NASA's combustion investigations (from its PSI database) to give fire safety insights for spacecraft, space stations, Moon and Mars bases, and pressurized rovers. The Moon base will likely use **30% oxygen or more**, and PSI has **no fire data at Moon gravity** yet.

**FlameSense** does exactly that, plus things other teams won't:
- **Investigations:** every NASA combustion study summarized (goal, fuels, conditions, what changed between tests, what was measured), ranked for the habitat you choose with a formula you can see and adjust, and compared side by side;
- **Evidence:** findings quoted from NASA documents, checked word for word, showing where studies agree or disagree;
- **Missions:** which materials are likely to burn in a Moon base or rover, with "bounds" between orbit and Earth results because Moon data doesn't exist yet;
- **Reality checks:** it predicted later NASA suppression tests (FLEX) without seeing them; it tested whether NASA's 2025 Moon-gravity fire results fall between orbit and Earth results; and it froze a public plan, before NASA's first fire tests on the Moon, for how it will check them. It also shows a gap nobody talks about: NASA's microgravity data on burning solids stops at 23% oxygen, and the Moon base will be at 30% or more;
- **Data:** researchers download model-ready data with a dictionary and citations;
- **Safety tech:** what the research means for prevention, detection and suppression, and NASA technology already used on Earth (water-mist extinguishers, smoke detectors, firefighter gear);
- plus the Gravity Twin (Earth flame vs. space flame) and a Fire Lab.

Learn this well enough to explain it to a friend in 30 seconds. You'll say it many times.

## 7. Usability test (adds Validity and UX points)

**Who:** 5 people who aren't developers (students, teachers, relatives aged 18+). Online call with screen sharing is fine.
**When:** Fri 13 Nov, 19:00–22:00, on the live link (book the people this week).
**How (20 minutes each):**
1. Send the link. Say: "We're testing the app, not you. Please think out loud."
2. **Baseline first (this gives you a measurable outcome):** ask them to find "the NASA investigation most relevant to fire safety in a Moon base" using only the PSI website (https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/search?q=combustion). Stop at 5 minutes. Record time and what they found. Then switch to FlameSense for the tasks below. For a fair test, give half the testers FlameSense first and the PSI site second.
3. Give three tasks in FlameSense, one at a time, and start a timer for each:
   - a) "Find the NASA investigation most relevant to a Moon base, and say why it ranks first."
   - b) "Find out what FlameSense predicted for NASA's Moon fire tests, and how it scored on the earlier ones."
   - c) "Open any test run and find which NASA source it came from."
4. Don't help unless they're stuck for 2 minutes (then the task counts as not completed).
5. Ask at the end: "What was confusing?" and "Would you use this in your studies or work? Why?"

Testers should match the statement's audience: engineering students (mechanical, aerospace, CSE), a teacher or professor, an engineer. Record each session in a sheet with columns `tester_type, order (PSI-first|FS-first), task, tool (PSI|FlameSense), completed (yes/no), seconds, comment`.

**Expert feedback (adoption evidence for Impact):** ask 1–2 people from the target audience (a mechanical or aerospace engineering professor at a Rajshahi university, or a Space Apps mentor) to try FlameSense for 15 minutes. Ask for one sentence of honest feedback and **written permission** to quote it with their name and role. Quote it exactly; never edit their words. Send the sheet to Ehsanur the same day. He adds the results to the app's Methods page, and the video quotes them. Never round results up.
