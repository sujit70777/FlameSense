## 1. The project in one minute

**Challenge:** Flame in Freefall: AI-Powered Fire Safety Insights from Microgravity Combustion Data.

**The story:** On Earth, rising hot air stretches a flame into an orange teardrop. In orbit nothing
rises, so flames turn round, blue and slow. NASA now plans Moon and Mars habitats with lower pressure and
**34% oxygen** (the air we breathe is 21%), to make spacewalks faster. More oxygen makes materials burn
more easily, and NASA has very little fire data at Moon or Mars gravity.

**FlameSense** is a web app that uses NASA's space-station fire experiment data to:
- show how a flame changes from Earth to orbit (Gravity Twin);
- predict whether a flame keeps burning and how fast it spreads (Fire Lab);
- map where flames survive, and where NASA has no data (Map);
- show which materials are likely to burn in a Moon habitat, and which experiments NASA should run next (Missions);
- answer questions from NASA documents with citations (Ask);
- explain what this means for fire safety on Earth, such as oxygen-rich hospital rooms (Earth lessons).

Learn this well enough to explain it to a friend in 30 seconds. You'll say it many times.

## 7. Usability test (adds Validity and UX points)

**Who:** 5 people who aren't developers (students, teachers, relatives aged 18+). Online call with screen sharing is fine.
**When:** Plan A: 9 Nov. Plan B: Fri 13 Nov, 19:00–22:00.
**How (20 minutes each):**
1. Send the link. Say: "We're testing the app, not you. Please think out loud."
2. Give three tasks, one at a time, and start a timer for each:
   - a) "Find out if a fabric is likely to keep burning on the space station."
   - b) "Find where NASA has no fire data at Moon gravity."
   - c) "Open any test run and find which NASA source it came from."
3. Don't help unless they're stuck for 2 minutes (then the task counts as not completed).
4. Ask at the end: "What was confusing?" and "Would you use this to learn about fire in space? Why?"

Record each session in a sheet with columns `tester, task, completed (yes/no), seconds, comment`. Send the sheet to Ehsanur the same day. He adds the results to the app's Methods page, and the video quotes them. Never round results up.
