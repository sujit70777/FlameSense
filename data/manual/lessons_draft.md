title_en: Apollo 1: pure oxygen let a small fire spread in seconds
space_finding_en: On 27 January 1967, during a launch-pad test with the hatches closed and the cabin pressurised with 100% oxygen, a flash fire swept through the Apollo 1 Command Module so quickly there was no time to open the hatches. Investigators found evidence of arcing in wires near the environmental control system, and found that the pure oxygen atmosphere made the fire spread quickly. The crew died from carbon monoxide and other toxic gases; their burns were likely survivable.
earth_meaning_en: Oxygen-rich air turns a small ignition into a fast fire, and smoke and toxic gas, not flames, can be what kills. The three-piece hatch took 90 seconds to open in ideal conditions and could not be opened under the raised pressure.
what_to_do_en: After the fire NASA restricted and controlled the amount and location of combustible material in the spacecraft, fitted a hatch that opens from inside or outside in three seconds, and designed new spacesuits with fire-resistant materials. The test had been treated as non-hazardous and was not reviewed by safety organisations, so there was no rescue process.
source: NASA Safety & Mission Assurance: Apollo 1 | https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/apollo-1

icon: history
title_en: Apollo 13: an overheated oxygen tank and damaged wire insulation
space_finding_en: On the way to the Moon in April 1970, oxygen tank number two exploded and damaged tank number one. Before launch, the tank heaters had been changed from 28 to 65 volts DC without updating their thermostatic switches; during a pad test they were left on and reached about 1000 °F, degrading the Teflon wire insulation inside the tank.
earth_meaning_en: The accident came from a combination of mistakes and a deficient design: one changed part, without the parts around it being checked, left wiring inside a pure-oxygen tank damaged. Wiring insulation added after Apollo 1 helped prevent arcing when the crew powered the Command Module back up.
what_to_do_en: The review board recommended redesigning and recertifying the oxygen storage system and reviewing every system that used cryogenic oxygen. Engineers added a third tank in a different bay, upgraded the thermostats, removed the stirring fans and sheathed all electrical wiring in stainless steel.
source: NASA Safety & Mission Assurance: Apollo 13 | https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/apollo-13

status: approved
icon: history
title_en: Mir 1997: an oxygen generator fire blocked an escape route
space_finding_en: On 24 February 1997 a solid-fuel oxygen generator canister on the Mir space station ignited, shooting a flame about three feet long with sparks and molten metal. It blocked the way to one of the two Soyuz escape vehicles and filled the modules with smoke. The crew put on oxygen masks and fought it with foam extinguishers; life support cleared the smoke over several hours.
earth_meaning_en: Equipment that makes or stores oxygen can itself become the fire source. Investigators found that hydrocarbons introduced into the canister raised the risk, most likely latex glove material that got into it during preparation on the ground.
what_to_do_en: Afterwards, crew training for fire emergencies and ground controllers' support were changed, and ground preparation of the canisters was improved. The International Space Station now has oxygen generation with dissimilar backups, and its solid-fuel generator has added safety measures.
source: NASA History: 25 Years Ago, Fire Aboard Space Station Mir | https://www.nasa.gov/history/25-years-ago-fire-aboard-space-station-mir/

title_en: A shuttle fire detector that tells smoke from dust
space_finding_en: The Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector, first used in space shuttle orbiters, samples the air for combustion particles and can tell them apart from other matter such as dust.
earth_meaning_en: Because it can tell combustion particles from dust, it is less likely to give false alarms, and it signals a fire condition before flame and smoke appear.
what_to_do_en: For large areas such as mines, many sensors can be linked to one central unit that warns of an early fire and shows where it is.
source: NASA Spinoff (1978): Fire Detector | https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9407

title_en: Lighter air tanks for firefighters
space_finding_en: NASA's Johnson Space Center and Scott Aviation developed a lightweight firefighter air-tank system: an aluminium liner wrapped in resin-impregnated glass fibre, pressurised to 4,500 psi.
earth_meaning_en: The backpack weighs 20 pounds for a 30-minute air supply, 13 pounds less than conventional tanks, and the composite wrap eliminates corrosion.
what_to_do_en: The design also improved the face mask for better vision and made the low-air warning personal, so only the firefighter wearing it hears it, which cuts confusion at a fire.
source: NASA Spinoff (1976): Fireman's Air Tanks | https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9582

title_en: Spacesuit materials in firefighter clothing
space_finding_en: Project FIRES, run by NASA and the U.S. Fire Administration with technical management from Marshall Space Flight Center, applied lightweight, fire-resistant, heat-protective materials first developed for astronauts' spacesuits and spacecraft thermal protection.
earth_meaning_en: The project aimed to update firefighter gear that did not adequately protect against many of the hazards met while fighting fires.
what_to_do_en: Fourteen municipal fire departments field-tested the new protective ensemble, and their data was to form the basis for new nationwide standards for protective clothing.
